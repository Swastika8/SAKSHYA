The art of
an unplanned Sunday.

A notebook, a comfortable pair of shoes and a morning with nowhere urgent to be.

By Ananya Rao6 min readWeekend notes

THIS WEEK'S NOTE

Notice the ordinary.

The best part of a small trip can be a familiar street seen a little differently.

I used to make a list for every weekend. Cafés to try, places to see, errands to finish. By Sunday evening I had plenty of photographs and the odd feeling that I had spent two days rushing.

Last month, I tried something simpler. I left the house early with a notebook and walked towards the old market. No route saved, no table booked. Just enough time to see where the morning went.

Start before the city is busy

A quiet street tells a different story. Shopkeepers straighten their displays. The first pot of tea goes on. A person waters a row of plants outside a doorway. There is room to stop without feeling in the way.

A good day does not always need a full itinerary.

Choose one small thing

I bought a warm breakfast, sat near a window and wrote down five things I had noticed. The colour of a sign. A dog asleep in a patch of light. The sound of cups being stacked. Nothing extraordinary, which was the point.

Leave a little room

Slow travel is not only about going far away. It can be an hour in your own neighbourhood, an afternoon in a nearby town or a train ride with time to look out of the window. Keep the plan small enough that you can change it.

I was home by lunchtime. The list of things I had done was short. The morning felt wonderfully long.
